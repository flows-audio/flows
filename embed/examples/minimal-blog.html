Why a filter sweep sounds like a vowel

A short note on lowpass filters

Say ooo, then slowly open your mouth into aaa. Your vocal cords did not change; the shape of your mouth decided which of their overtones got through. A lowpass filter does the same thing to a synthesizer: it keeps the low partials of a bright saw wave and lets the high ones go.

Play a chord below, then drag Cutoff while it rings. Lower values sound closed and round, like ooo. Raise Resonance and the edge of the filter starts to sing on its own pitch, which is the moment a sweep starts to sound like a voice.

Filters are where most of a sound's character lives. The oscillator decides what raw material exists; the filter decides how much of it you hear, and its envelope decides how that changes over the life of a note.
